# Test on real frames before polishing pixels

The changes that will help most are not new rendering effects. They are, in order: (1) fixing training settings that can silently destroy a 50-pixel flag (default left-right flips, resizing the 4K frame to 640, double scale jitter, tiles without overlap); (2) fixing the class list, because the only public report from another ICMTC 2026 team says the briefed flags are Egypt, Iraq, Syria and Yemen, and three of those are missing from our 28 classes; (3) building a small real test set and training recipe around it, because published studies show that 50 real images can be worth 400 and that most synthetic-side improvements vanish once real fine-tuning exists; and only then (4) a short list of generator upgrades with good evidence: real video compression, a camera-ordered blur/noise/sharpen chain applied to the flag *and* the ground around it, a mix of blending modes, occlusion and truncation, and distractors rendered through the same pipeline as the flags. The risk is concrete: the other ICMTC team's YOLO11n found a flag-shaped object almost every time (class-agnostic recall 0.89-1.00), but gave Egypt the right label with confidence only 23% of the time at a 0.25 threshold and 0/30 times at 0.6 ([Maher-star-eng report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Maher-star-eng/ICMTC-2026/main/icmtc/training/resolution_and_remap_report.md)). Our generator already covers scale, rotation, folds, shadows, camera matching and negatives well, so the remaining gains are in the recipe, the labels and the test. Throughout this report, **"measured"** means a number from a published experiment (often in a different domain), and **"estimate"** means arithmetic or judgement from our research notes; no number here was measured on our own data or on the SIYI A8 mini.

## Five training settings can erase the flag before the generator matters

Start with the pixel arithmetic, because it decides everything else. The SIYI A8 mini records 4096x2160 video with an 81° horizontal field of view, and **video is H.265 only, at 20 Mbps for 4K** ([SIYI A8 mini spec](https://siyi.biz/en/product/tri-axis-single-camera-gimbal/a8-mini/spec/)). From those numbers (estimate), the ground sample distance at nadir is about **2.1 cm/px at 50 m and 4.2 cm/px at 100 m**, so a 2 m flag is about 96 px long at 50 m and 48 px long at 100 m. The table shows what the model actually sees under different pipelines (estimate, from our notes).

| Pipeline | Flag size seen by the model (length x width) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Full 4K frame resized to imgsz=640 | about 8-16 x 4-8 px | Too small; the Egypt eagle becomes 1-3 px |
| Full frame at imgsz=1280 | about 15-32 x 7-16 px | Borderline; a P2 head might help here |
| Full frame at imgsz=1920 | about 22-48 x 11-23 px | Cheapest option worth testing |
| Native-resolution tiles (tile=640) | about 45-95 x 22-47 px | Best detail, highest cost |

The two candidates to compare on the Jetson are therefore a full frame at imgsz=1920 and native tiling at 640. Ultralytics now has a built-in `tile` argument for train, val and predict, with **20% default overlap, orientation-preserving OBB clipping, rotated NMS across seams, and removal of clipped instances that keep less than 10% of their area** ([Ultralytics tiling guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/high-resolution-tiling)). Neither choice wins everywhere (measured, by Ultralytics): on VisDrone, a full frame at imgsz=1920 scored **23.7 mAP at 5.8 ms versus 21.7 mAP at 13.5 ms** for tile=640, while on DOTA OBB, tile=1024 scored **64.4 mAP versus 61.1** for imgsz=4096 ([Ultralytics tiling guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/high-resolution-tiling)). On a Jetson Orin NX, YOLO26n at 640 takes **4.13 ms in FP16 TensorRT** (measured, model time only) ([Ultralytics Jetson guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)); scaling that linearly gives roughly 37 ms for imgsz=1920 and roughly 132 ms for 32 native 640 tiles (estimate; measure it on the device). Two other ICMTC teams reached the same conclusion independently: one runs at imgsz 1920 by default because a 2 m flag is "~6-10 px" at 640 ([zakariahmedd34 repo](https://github.com/zakariahmedd34/uav-gps-localization)), and a SUAS team on a Jetson Orin NX reported that reducing input size "hampers" small-object detection ([UAS 2030 ODLC page](https://uas2030.github.io/docs/suas2025/object-detection-localisation-and-classification/)).

Our generator already writes 640 tiles, so check three things in that code. First, **tiles need overlap of at least the largest flag**, about 105 px for a 95 x 47 px flag at 45° (estimate); Roboflow's Grid tiling mode has **no overlap setting at all** and does not document how it handles cut boxes ([Roboflow preprocessing docs](https://docs.roboflow.com/datasets/dataset-versions/image-preprocessing)), so do not re-tile the uploaded batch with Grid. Second, keep a label on a cut flag only if roughly 40-60% of it is visible (judgement); Ultralytics' 10% rule keeps slivers that are just one colour band. Third, **watch the empty-tile share**: with 1-3 flags per frame, most tiles are empty, roughly 80-90% for a 3x4 grid (estimate), far above the 0-10% background share in Ultralytics' guidance ([Ultralytics training tips](https://docs.ultralytics.com/yolov5/tutorials/tips_for_best_training_results/)). Subsample empty tiles to about 10-20%, and keep mostly the ones that contain distractors.

The augmentation defaults are the most dangerous single setting. **Ultralytics flips images left-right with probability 0.5 by default (`fliplr=0.5`)**, and its docs say to disable it "where left-right orientation carries meaning" ([Ultralytics augmentation guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/yolo-data-augmentation/)). A mirrored flag is not a real flag, so set `fliplr=0` and `flipud=0`; a 180° turn is physically valid and already comes from the generator's 0-360° rotation. For the same reason set `degrees=0` (or at most 10-15), because rotating box labels at train time only produces looser boxes. Set `scale` to about 0.2-0.3 instead of the default 0.5, since the 50-100 m altitude range already doubles the flag size range, and the default would push some flags down to about 11 px wide (estimate). Keep `hsv_h` at 0.015 or lower, because hue is the class (red versus orange, black versus dark green), and keep mosaic with the default `close_mosaic=10`. Use **OBB labels**: a 2:1 flag at 45° fills only about 44% of its axis-aligned box (estimate), and Ultralytics' tiling supports OBB end to end. On DOTA, YOLO26n-obb to YOLO26s-obb adds 2.0 mAP50, while s to m adds only 0.1 ([Ultralytics OBB docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/obb/)), so YOLO26s-obb (or YOLO11s-obb) is the natural Jetson choice. A P2 (stride-4) head is only worth trying if you run full frames at 1280 or below ([Ultralytics discussion #8227](https://github.com/orgs/ultralytics/discussions/8227)). Finally, **validate on untiled frames** with `val(tile=True)`, which merges predictions before scoring against the original labels, and re-validate the deployed engine on the Jetson, because INT8 cost about 1-3 mAP points in Ultralytics' tests ([Ultralytics Jetson guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)).

## Fifty real frames beat another month of rendering

The strongest pattern across the literature is that a small amount of real data matters more than synthetic polish. In a UAV study with synthetic aerial data (measured, DeepGTA-UAV), better rendering raised synthetic-only VisDrone mAP from 8.4 to 10.2 but made **no difference after real fine-tuning (45.1 versus 45.0)**, and time-of-day matching raised SeaDronesSee from 10.5 to 14.6 synthetic-only but only from 60.3 to 60.5 after fine-tuning ([Kiefer et al., ICPR 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.12252)). A UAV YOLO study found that **fine-tuning a synthetic-pretrained model on 50 real images matched a model that needed 400 real images** ([CEUR-WS paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4155/paper03.pdf)). In cut-and-paste training, 10% of the real data plus synthetic scored 43.2 AP, beating 100% real data alone at 41.9 ([Dwibedi et al., Cut, Paste and Learn](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.01642)), and RarePlanes reached roughly 91-96% of real-only performance with about 10% of the real data ([RarePlanes, WACV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shermeyer_RarePlanes_Synthetic_Data_Takes_Flight_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)). The same studies also show that synthetic validation mAP is not a guide to real performance: synthetic-only VisDrone was 10.2 against 43.9 for real-only ([Kiefer et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.12252)).

So the team needs three real test sets that are never used for training. Set A is rooftop footage of small printed flags, scaled so the pixel size matches: a 30 cm flag filmed from 15 m has the same size-to-distance ratio as a 2 m flag at 100 m, and from 7.5 m it matches 50 m (estimate). It tests printing, colour and emblem detail, but not haze, desert glare, fabric wrinkles or H.265 at 20 Mbps, and printed paper colour differs from dyed polyester. Set B is the most valuable: even 20-50 frames of a few real 2 m flags on sand, filmed at 50-100 m with the A8 mini itself. Set C is real empty desert and airfield footage, used to count false positives per image. On every set, report mAP50 and per-class recall first (mAP50-95 punishes small-object localisation too much), recall by altitude bin, the normalised confusion matrix, and false positives per image. The generator should write per-instance metadata (altitude, pixel size, yaw, occlusion, distance to tile seam) so these bins can be computed offline. Pick the Egypt confidence threshold from the precision-recall curve, not the default 0.25. The ICMTC team that did not test on real footage got **zero detections on its own synthetic test frames** and concluded that "a real recording is still needed" ([Maher-star-eng dry run](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Maher-star-eng/ICMTC-2026/main/icmtc/training/dry_run_report.md)).

The training schedule follows from this. Train on synthetic data from COCO-pretrained weights without freezing the backbone (freezing cost up to 13.5% in a domain-randomization study, 66.4 versus 78.1 AP) ([Tremblay et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06516)); then fine-tune on rooftop and field frames with about 10-30% synthetic images mixed in so classes without real examples are not forgotten (estimate). Two studies prefer pretrain-then-fine-tune over mixing ([Nowruzi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07061v1); [Kiefer et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.12252)), but another found mixed sets with 5-20% real data cut real-data needs by up to 70% ([Burdorf et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00632v1)), so try both once real data exists. Do not overproduce synthetic images either: performance saturated at about 10K images with pretrained weights in the NVIDIA study ([Tremblay et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06516)). After the first model exists, run it on unlabelled real empty footage, collect its false positives, and add those frames as negatives and those crops as paste-in distractors; Ultralytics' community guidance recommends exactly this loop ([Ultralytics community](https://community.ultralytics.com/t/how-does-yolov8-reduce-false-recognition-how-does-it-reduce-false-recognition-of-similar-objects/1143)). Use per-component ablations (turn off folds, shadows, look transfer, distractors or the blend mix one at a time, same seed and image count, 2-3 seeds each) against sets A and B to learn which generator features actually help; this is the standard protocol in the cut-paste and domain-randomization papers ([Dwibedi et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.01642); [Tremblay et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06516)).

## The class list does not match the likely mission

No official ICMTC rulebook could be retrieved; the official UAVC site refused connections. Two independent 2026 team repositories agree (unofficially) on 2 m x 1 m flags lying flat on a desert airfield, flight at 50-100 m, 2 flags plus 1 bonus flag, full localisation points within 20 m and partial credit to 60 m, and **flag identity worth 10 points** ([zakariahmedd34 repo](https://github.com/zakariahmedd34/uav-gps-localization)). One of them restricts its outputs to four "briefed" flags: **Egypt, Iraq, Syria and Yemen** ([Maher-star-eng README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Maher-star-eng/ICMTC-2026/main/icmtc/README.md)). This is one team's statement, not an official list, and the team should confirm it with the organisers. But it fits the evidence on what goes wrong: all four are red-white-black horizontal tricolours told apart only by the central emblem, and that team's model, trained on 27,812 images and 196 classes at 640 px, gave the correct class with confidence 0.6 for **Egypt 0/30, Iraq 0/31, Yemen 2/28 and Syria 10/31**, even though class-agnostic recall at 0.25 was 0.89-1.00. Raising the input to 1280 helped Yemen and Syria but left Egypt and Iraq flat ([Maher-star-eng report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Maher-star-eng/ICMTC-2026/main/icmtc/training/resolution_and_remap_report.md)).

For our list this is a serious gap (inference). Among our 28 classes, Egypt is the only red-white-black horizontal tricolour, and it makes up 60% of the flag images. A model trained this way has no reason to look at the emblem: "red over white over black" is enough to score perfectly on our data. A real Iraqi, Yemeni or Syrian flag will then most likely be reported as Egypt with high confidence. **Add `iraq`, `yemen` and `syria` as classes now**, and render Syria in both versions: since March 2025 the national flag is **green-white-black with three red stars**, replacing the red-white-black flag with two green stars ([FOTW Syria](https://crwflags.com/fotw/flags/sy.html); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/program/newsfeed/2025/4/25/syrias-new-three-starred-flag-raised-at-un)), and the organisers could print either. Two separate class names (for example `syria` and `syria_old`) that map to the same answer are safer than one class with two looks. Give the four briefed flags most of the sampling weight, oversample the confusable group 2-3x relative to other classes, and keep other tricolours (Sudan, Jordan, Palestine, Libya, plus UAE and Germany already in the list) as hard negatives or extra classes, so the model must read the emblem (our suggestion, not tested). Render emblems at true ground resolution and then degrade them; at native resolution the Egypt eagle is only about 7-16 px tall (estimate). A "mission subset" setting in the generator config makes it cheap to switch weights once the real list is known. At inference, the other team masks outputs to the briefed classes and uses a lower threshold for them (0.25 versus 0.60 for others) ([Maher-star-eng README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Maher-star-eng/ICMTC-2026/main/icmtc/README.md)); that works only if the list is known before the flight.

On 180-degree ambiguity, our own check of the flag designs (not from a source) finds **no pair inside the 28-class list that is an exact 180° rotation of another**, so no merge is needed today. The risk is outside the list. Poland rotated 180° is red over white, which is the flag of Indonesia and Monaco; on the ground at any heading these cannot be told apart, so never add a 180° twin of a class as a hard negative, because the labels would contradict each other. The same rule applies to the new classes: before adding any distractor flag, check that it is not a 180° turn of a class. If flags can also lie face-down (mirrored print-through), mirror twins join the problem (for example Ireland and Côte d'Ivoire); whether organisers ever do this is unverified, so do not add flips until it is confirmed. The list does contain look-alike groups where only stripe direction or a small symbol differs on a 2:1 sheet, and these deserve oversampling and a close look at the confusion matrix: France versus Netherlands (same colours; stripes across versus along the long side), Germany versus Belgium, Denmark versus Switzerland versus Norway (red with a white cross), Sweden versus Ukraine (blue and yellow), and Egypt versus UAE (red, white, black, green). Switzerland's real flag is square, so check how the organisers would print it on a 2 m x 1 m sheet.

If the confusion matrix shows that identity, not detection, is the main error, which is what both the other ICMTC team and the FAIR1M aerial benchmark found ([FAIR1M](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.05569)), switch to two stages: a single-class "flag" detector, then a small classifier on rectified crops taken from the original 4K frame. The classifier sees up to 2-6x more emblem pixels than a detector running at reduced input size, and it can be trained on huge numbers of cheap synthetic crops (estimate). The generator only needs to also export rectified per-instance crops with their labels. Scoring supports this order of work: localisation within 20 m is worth more than identity, and a human can read the submitted crops as a backup ([zakariahmedd34 repo](https://github.com/zakariahmedd34/uav-gps-localization)), so recall on "is there a flag here" comes first.

## Six generator upgrades with real evidence behind them

**Compression comes first.** At 20 Mbps and 25 fps over 4096x2160, the A8 mini gets about **0.09 bits per pixel** (estimate from the spec), which smooths sand texture and smears thin stripes and emblems inside a 50 px flag; 4:2:0 chroma subsampling halves the colour resolution, which hits thin red and green details hardest. Small objects suffer most from compression, and compression-based augmentation helps ([arXiv 2211.05805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.05805)). If deployment uses video frames, run real x265 through ffmpeg on short 3-8 frame clips with small shifts (`-c:v libx265 -crf 28..38 -pix_fmt yuv420p`) and keep the middle frame; a cheaper proxy is chroma downsample/upsample, a light bilateral filter and JPEG at quality 50-75. For stills, re-encode with JPEG at quality 70-95. Always compress a region that contains both the flag and the ground; compressing only the flag leaves a learnable seam. Decide first whether the competition pipeline uses video frames or JPEG stills, and match it.

**Second, apply the camera chain in camera order, on a region that includes the background.** The order is: supersampled render, downsample with `INTER_AREA`, PSF blur with sigma drawn from about 0.6-1.2 px (estimate, from diffraction at F2.8 and the small sensor), signal-dependent noise whose strength and blotchiness are fitted to flat sand in the same photo, then an unsharp mask (k about 0.2-1.0) to copy the camera's sharpening halos, then compression ([rendering notes, Brooks et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Brooks_Unprocessing_Images_for_Learned_Raw_Denoising_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf)). Do shading in linear light, not in gamma space. This extends the existing camera matching rather than replacing it. Physically based sensor effects of this kind raised synthetic-to-real car detection on KITTI from **54.60 to 61.88 AP50** and from 46.83 to 51.24 (measured) ([Carlson et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.07721)).

**Third, mix blending modes.** In cut-and-paste training (measured, indoor objects, Faster R-CNN), no blending scored 65.9, Gaussian blur only 68.9, Poisson only 58.4, and **rendering each object with all modes as separate samples scored 72.4**, about +8 over no blending ([Dwibedi et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.01642)). Our single "feather plus small Gaussian blur" path is close to the Gaussian-only row. Randomly choose per flag among a hard edge, Gaussian feathering at several widths, slight alpha erosion or dilation, and motion-blurred alpha. Do not use Poisson blending (`cv2.seamlessClone`) as a main mode: it was the worst single mode and pulls flag colours toward the sand. A 2026 small-UAV study (measured) found that a nano YOLO-class model overfits to the "unnatural edges" of pasted objects and that copy-paste gave high precision but low recall ([alphaXiv 2604.19999](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.19999.md)), and the UAV Austin SUAS team reports the same edge-artifact problem ([Hawk-Eye docs](https://uavaustin.github.io/hawk-eye/data_generation/overview.html)).

**Fourth, add occlusion and truncation.** In the same ablation, removing occlusion cost **10.6 AP**, the largest single factor, and removing truncation cost 1.9 (measured) ([Dwibedi et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.01642)). For flags on sand this means sand drifted over edges (replace flag pixels with the ground pixels underneath), 10-30 cm stones at corners (3-15 px) with sun-aligned shadows, a folded-over corner showing a lighter mirrored print, and shadows of people, poles or vehicles that cross both the flag and the ground. Put the same stones and shadows on empty ground too, so they do not become a flag-only cue, and recompute the OBB from the final visible area.

**Fifth, extend distractors to the photo-background path and render them through the identical pipeline.** Distractors added 2.5 AP in cut-and-paste training and about 1.1 AP in domain randomization (measured) ([Dwibedi et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.01642); [Tremblay et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06516)). The key point is that if only flags get folds, contact shadows, feathered edges and matched noise, the detector learns "anything with pasted-object statistics is a flag". Good candidates are single-colour tarps, striped mats, banners, vehicle shapes, runway paint, and tricolour "anti-flags" in non-target colours. Also leave some real flags almost unprocessed (taut, no folds, no shadow) so folds are not the cue either.

**Sixth, make lighting and colour local.** In domain randomization, fixed instead of random lighting cost 6.1 AP, while global brightness/contrast jitter was worth only 0.1 AP (measured) ([Tremblay et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.06516)). Our global look transfer handles the overall style, but not position: multiply the flag by a heavily smoothed map of local background brightness (this captures vignetting, cloud shadow and exposure falloff), darken and blue-shift the flag where it overlaps a ground shadow using a ratio measured from that photo, and drive fold shading from one sun direction per background (a 5-second manual click on one shadow per photo is the most reliable method). Add per-flag fading, printer colour shifts and sand dust in Lab space; these magnitudes are judgement values, since no measured fading data for sublimated polyester was found ([textiles.org](https://www.textiles.org/?p=1458)).

Two dataset-level changes belong with these. Ultralytics recommends **at least 1,500 images and 10,000 labelled instances per class** ([Ultralytics training tips](https://docs.ultralytics.com/yolov5/tutorials/tips_for_best_training_results/)); our 500-image batch has roughly 1,000 instances in total, and with Egypt at 60% the other 27 classes get only about 15 each (estimate). Since synthetic flags are free, raise density to about 8-20 flags per frame (keeping some sparse frames) and generate about 5-15k frames (estimate). Background variety probably matters more than image count: the cut-and-paste work used 1,548 backgrounds about four times each ([Dwibedi et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.01642)), and open desert imagery is available from OpenAerialMap (CC-BY 4.0, but check each image) and USGS drone orthomosaics (public domain) ([AWS OpenAerialMap](https://registry.opendata.aws/openaerialmap/); [USGS EROS UAS](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-unmanned-aircraft-systems-uas-orthoimagery)). Sample altitude, gimbal pitch and time of day from the expected competition conditions; matching camera angle raised synthetic-only mAP from 29.2 to 36.6 in the DeepGTA-UAV study, though not after fine-tuning ([Kiefer et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.12252)).

Some effects are not worth the time at this pixel size (estimate). Rolling shutter skews a flag by only about 0.2-0.5 px, and heat shimmer on a mostly vertical 50-100 m path is sub-pixel. Motion blur is about 0.1 px in bright sun at roughly 1/3200 s, so add it rarely (about 20% of images, 0.5-3 px) and only when the background shows the same blur. Do not run CycleGAN or diffusion img2img over labelled flags: diffusion methods hallucinate even with control inputs, so outputs can stop matching the labels ([arXiv 2605.02291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02291)), and a model that recolours one stripe turns one country into another. If used at all, use them only for new backgrounds and distractors.

| 6 | H.265 / JPEG compression and camera-ordered PSF, noise, sharpening on regions that include ground | Generator | 1-3 days | +4 to +7 AP50 (measured, KITTI) |
| 7 | Blend-mode mix; occlusion and truncation | Generator | 1-2 days | +8 AP and 10.6 AP (measured, indoor objects) |
| 8 | Distractors through the same pipeline; hard-negative mining from real empty footage | Generator / recipe | 2-3 days | +2.5 and +1.1 AP (measured) |
| 9 | Fine-tune on 50-200 real frames with 10-30% synthetic mixed in | Training | 1 day after data | 50 real matched 400 (measured, UAV YOLO) |
| 10 | Local lighting, sun-consistent shading, fading and dust | Generator | 2-4 days | Lighting -6.1 AP when fixed (measured); parameters estimated |
| 11 | Two-stage detector plus crop classifier, only if the confusion matrix shows identity errors | Model | 3-5 days | Adjacent domains (FAIR1M); no flag-specific study |

Ranks 1-3 cost little and remove ways to fail outright; rank 4 is what makes every later change measurable. The measured gains in ranks 6-10 come from cars, kitchens and other UAV targets, not from 50-pixel flags, so treat them as reasons to try a change, then keep it only if it helps on sets A and B.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The generator is already more physically careful than most published competition pipelines, and the evidence says extra realism has diminishing returns once even a few dozen real frames exist. The larger risks are elsewhere: a flip setting that teaches mirrored flags, a downscale that removes the emblem, and a class list where "red-white-black" can only mean Egypt, which means the briefed look-alikes would be confidently mislabelled. The other ICMTC team's numbers show that the hard part of this task is not finding the flag but naming it, and that pushes the team toward emblem-level detail (native-resolution crops, compression-aware rendering of small emblems) and toward the Iraq-Syria-Yemen group as the real "focus class", not Egypt alone.

The most useful next step is therefore cheap and physical: print a few flags, film them from a rooftop and, if at all possible, lay two real 2 m flags on sand and fly the A8 mini over them. That small real set turns every recommendation above from a guess into a measurement, and it is the only way to know which of the generator's many effects is actually earning its place.
